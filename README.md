## Overview

Belphegor is a major demon in Christian demonology, derived from the Hebrew Bible's Baal-Peor (בַּעַל פְּעוֹר), a Moabite deity worshipped on Mount Peor in Numbers 25:1–5 and condemned in Hosea 9:10 and Psalm 106:28. The English form Belphegor reaches us through Latin Belphegor / Beelphegor and entered demonological literature through medieval and early-modern grimoires. Peter Binsfeld's Tractatus de confessionibus maleficorum et sagarum (1589) names Belphegor the chief demon of sloth (acedia) in the seven-deadly-sins / seven-princes-of-Hell schema. In the slightly earlier Lanterne of Light (a 15th-century English Lollard text), he is paired with gluttony rather than sloth. Jacques Collin de Plancy's Dictionnaire infernal (1818, illustrated 1863) calls him Hell's ambassador to France, makes Mary Magdalene his enemy, and depicts him as a horned, bearded male figure or, in a separate tradition, as a woman. Niccolò Machiavelli's novella The Story of Belfagor the Archdevil (Belfagor arcidiavolo, c. 1518–1527) sends Belphegor to Earth to investigate marriage; Pierre de Larivey adapted the story in 1607. The 1965 French novel and 1965 ORTF television series Belphégor, le fantôme du Louvre revived the figure in modern popular culture.

## Etymology

- **Origin**: From Hebrew Baʿal-Pəʿôr (בַּעַל-פְּעוֹר), 'Lord of Peor' — Peor being a place in Moab where this deity was worshipped (Num 25). Latinized in late antiquity and the Middle Ages as Belphegor.
- **Meaning**: Literally 'Lord of (the place) Peor'. Some demonological writers (including the modern Wikipedia gloss) translate Pəʿôr as 'gap', yielding 'Lord of the Gap', but this gloss is not universally accepted in biblical scholarship.
- **First use**: 13th century BCE setting in the Hebrew Bible (Num 25); demon-name in Latin Christian sources from the early modern period; foregrounded in Peter Binsfeld's 1589 demon classification

## Theories & Explanations

### Paranormal / Cryptozoological

**Demon of sloth** _(Peter Binsfeld (1589), Jacques Collin de Plancy (1818))_

Belphegor is a real spiritual being who tempts humans to acedia / sloth, one of the seven princes of Hell.

### Scientific

**Demonization of a Moabite deity** _(Mainstream biblical scholars)_

Modern biblical scholarship treats Belphegor as a Christian demonization of the Moabite deity Baal-Peor, following the standard pattern of converting rival biblical gods into demons.

### Skeptical

**Literary and theological construct** _(Modern religious-studies scholars)_

Belphegor is a literary and demonological construct without empirical referent; a useful figure for moral, narrative, and esoteric purposes rather than a phenomenon to be investigated.
